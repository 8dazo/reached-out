# Outreach Message Quality v2

## Objective
Write messages that read as a specific engineer reaching out to a specific engineering team. Never mail-merge a fixed paragraph, invent company research or praise, or sacrifice relevance for 30/day volume.

## Required source-grounded preparation
For each candidate record the current job URL and quote/paraphrase of the exact role requirement, current organization product/technical evidence, why Devansh's experience genuinely overlaps, precise contact role, and one personalized hook. If a company-specific hook is missing, hold the lead and research instead of sending an empty generic message.

## Choose ONE angle for each contact
- Problem-first: highlight a concrete systems challenge actually identified in the JD, then connect to Captar or production architecture.
- Builder-to-builder: for engineering founders or CTOs, mention a directly related shipped technical project and the specific trade-off learned.
- Product-outcome: describe a feature, user problem or measurable product outcome relevant to the employer.
- Founder-led startup: concise note on 0→1 ownership and why the startup's live hiring need matches.
- Role-specific contractor: acknowledge exact international contract terms and relate deliverable ownership.
- Recruiter application: explicit exact job title and match in two direct bullets or short prose; include resume where official email application requests it.

## Composition constraints
- Subject: roughly 4–8 natural words, role or technical problem-specific; don't always repeat candidate name/job title. No clickbait or misleading claims.
- Opening: specific company/role observation from linked evidence. Avoid repeated 'I came across', 'I was interested', 'I saw your role' phrasing across consecutive messages.
- Body: around 75–115 words (shorter when appropriate), 2–3 tight paragraphs, one primary supporting accomplishment. Select from *accurate* resume-based claims: Stride.AI agent/backend production; Captar runtime policy, guardrails/traces/evals; API scale; Baelys AI image editor; 0→1 full-stack features. Never cram multiple generic proof points into every email.
- CTA: one low-friction, audience-specific ask; do not always write 'Would you be open to a brief discussion?' With recruiter ask about exact opening/consideration; engineering leader ask relevant project/role connection; founder ask whether the team is looking for this capability. Do not assume direct access to decision-maker.
- Signature: simple name and portfolio hyperlink; include GitHub when technically relevant, don't repeat a link block identical to every email. Mention living in India only when it clarifies confirmed remote eligibility; never make location a centerpiece unnecessarily.
- No fabricated familiarity, inflated claims, deceptive urgency, fake compliments, or unverified statements about tech stack, funding, team culture or remote hiring.
- Never claim a remote-India role from 'global' alone without explicit eligibility evidence.
- Follow ethical cold outreach requirements: relevant professional contact, truthful identity, simple opt-out honored immediately; no aggressive sequence.

## Variation checks BEFORE sending
1. Compare draft against prior 10 initial outreach emails. If opening construction, proof sequence, or CTA is highly similar, rewrite from another authentic angle.
2. Every email must contain at least one observation grounded in the current role/company source AND a concrete personal connection to that observation.
3. No more than one recipient at a company unless documented exception; preserve existing full Gmail history.
4. Draft quality gate: clear in 5 seconds what role, why match, why this person; remove fluff, adjectives, filler.
5. Retain draft angle, specific hook, evidence URL, and approved message body in lead record/checkpoint for audit.
6. Sending, failure, and dedupe safeguards from OUTREACH_V7.md remain mandatory.

## Priority
Send fewer but genuinely relevant messages instead of generic emails. A recipient should not be able to replace their company name with 20 other company names while leaving the rest of the message intact.


## Human builder-to-builder voice — v3 (effective immediately)
Inspired by the user's provided example email: write as a real engineer, with genuine motivation, details of what they built, and willingness to own early-stage work. Do NOT copy the example's hyperbole, hobbies, 5-year experience claim or personal facts. Never impersonate the author of the example.

- Start with a brief, natural TL;DR *only when it genuinely helps*, e.g. "Short version: I build agent infrastructure and I'd love to work on [verified specific company challenge]." Don't require literal "tldr;" in every email.
- Open with sincere interest in a *verified* product/problem/team detail. Prefer "I've been following..." only if true; otherwise "I read about..." or "Your work on..." with source.
- Use first-person, conversational sentences. Avoid recruiter-speak: "aligns with my background", "opportunity caught my attention", "appreciate your focus", "leverage", "my experience might fit", "short conversation about how my experience might fit".
- Give actual builder evidence and agency: "I built Captar because...", "I owned the backend and UI for...", "I care about getting it into production" (only when truthful). Tailor one to three relevant details from the resume, not a laundry list. Technical specificity beats adjective-heavy claims.
- Include a real personal reason for preferring lean, hands-on teams and owning work end-to-end, only when appropriate to the company and not asserted about the company without evidence. It's okay to show enthusiasm or slightly imperfect natural diction but do NOT invent vulnerabilities, hobbies, or unusual confessions.
- CTA should directly ask whether they are hiring, who owns the role, or whether the sender can show relevant work. Vary it naturally. No formulaic “Would you be open to a brief discussion?”.
- Length is flexible, typically 100–170 words for founder/builder-led emails where actual detail earns the length; 65–110 for recruiters. Prior 70–110 target is superseded for builder-led messages. Up to three short paragraphs plus links; no giant wall of text.
- Subjects should feel personal and unpolished-in-a-good-way: "Engineering at [company]", "Loved the way you're approaching [verified problem]", "Building [relevant field] at [company]" rather than a generic CV title, assuming evidence supports it.
- Uniqueness test: delete the company name. If message still works for 10 other companies, rewrite with verified product detail. Run a similarity check against 10 recent sent emails; vary narrative, sentence length, hook, and ask, not just vocabulary.
- Don't claim location flexibility that isn't independently supported. Keep verification and anti-duplicate rules unchanged.
- Before send, save source-grounded individualized draft and named reason why it differs from previous send templates.
